# Guide and reconciliation

Read for synthesizing an interview or reconciling independently captured founder perspectives.

## One founder's guide

Title the result `{Brand} Brand Discovery — Independent {Participant} Perspective`. Report the target and answered, unknown, skipped and pending counts. State whether the question sequence is completed or partial; either way, the brand interpretation remains provisional until the relevant people review it and audience assumptions are tested.

Use these shared section names unless the user requests another structure:

1. Founder perspective and founding purpose
2. Vision and definition of success
3. Audience and customer evidence
4. Positioning, alternatives and proof
5. Values and behaviours
6. Brand voice and contextual tone
7. Language guide and examples
8. Social media content pillars
9. Sample social content
10. Visual direction and content operations
11. Boundaries, claims and approval
12. Uncertainties, tensions and validation needs
13. Reusable AI brand brief
14. Evidence map
15. Interview archive
16. Handoff for combining founder interviews

Keep empty or unsupported sections visible as gaps rather than filling them with generic facts. Scale the length to the evidence and the user's request. If splitting a long guide, label the parts and missing sections; do not call the package complete before required material has been delivered.

## Make the result actionable

- Draft purpose, vision and positioning statements from the respondent's answers and label them proposed. Describe current experience separately from intended future identity.
- Define voice traits through observable choices: language, sentence structure, rhythm, humour, formality and emotional intensity. Keep context-dependent tone distinct from the underlying voice. Include use/avoid examples, generic-to-branded rewrites and relevant situation examples.
- Derive content pillars from audience needs and available evidence. For each include purpose, topics, format, proof or evidence gap, and an appropriate call to action. Do not promise engagement or business outcomes from a founder's approval.
- By default, provide three caption drafts and one short Reel script to demonstrate the voice. Honour a different requested format. Use supported business facts and explicit placeholders where details are missing; do not invent offers, prices, credentials, reviews or results.
- Record realistic resources, channels, production capacity, approval and measurement. Apply supplied brand-specific media policies rather than a universal rule about footage or AI imagery.
- Identify unverified claims and relevant privacy, consent and approval issues. Source verification is separate from founder preference and AI review. If sources/tools are unavailable, leave the claim unverified rather than adding confident language.
- Produce a compact AI brief containing the supported draft decisions, voice examples, content directions, boundaries and open choices. A partial brief must remain labelled partial.

## Evidence map and archive

For every major conclusion, identify participant-qualified question IDs, available sources, evidence type and remaining uncertainty. Founder statements about customers are reported evidence until independently substantiated. A supplied testimonial can support what its author said; it does not alone establish a universal treatment outcome or market preference.

If a claim is available only in a compact working brief, cite the actual checkpoint/summary plus its reported question ID, for example "Checkpoint 2 summary, referencing Founder A:Q008; raw reply unavailable." Do not label it a direct inspected quote. Report counters from a supplied compact checkpoint as checkpoint-reported unless the accessible records independently establish them.

Include exact accessible question/reply events, follow-ups and revisions. Compact checkpoint summaries cannot be expanded into a verbatim archive. If raw blocks are missing, report which ones and request them only when necessary. You may deliver a clearly labelled partial guide supported by available summaries; do not claim a complete archive or complete evidence review.

## Reconcile independent perspectives

Do this only when the user requests comparison and supplies both perspectives. If one is absent, ask for the missing report or provide a scoped review of the available one. Do not invent the other founder's position.

Align reports by theme, not question number. Use a comparison table with:

| Theme | Perspective A with evidence | Perspective B with evidence | Relationship | Decision or validation needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Tag each perspective's evidence as current delivery, intended future, preference or unknown, and distinguish a raw reply from a supplied summary. Shared aspirations remain aspirations. When a possible disagreement depends on exact wording or question context that is absent, mark it tentative and request the specific underlying excerpts before treating it as a definitive conflict or approved resolution. This does not prevent a useful preliminary comparison.

For more than two participants, extend the comparison to preserve each person's evidence and unresolved position; do not substitute majority voting for analysis or approval.

Classify the relationship as shared principle, complementary emphasis, compatible contextual difference, material disagreement, or insufficient evidence. Do not manufacture conflicts out of different wording. Conversely, do not blend incompatible audience, price, promise or strategic choices into vague compromise language.

For material disagreements, state the strongest credible reasoning on each side, any evidence advantage, realistic alternatives, consequences and what could resolve the question. Offer a recommendation when justified but leave founder decisions explicit. A context-specific tone rule can reconcile compatible preferences; it cannot resolve an unsupported strategic conflict by itself.

The combined guide must mark proposals, unresolved choices and founder-confirmed decisions separately. Compatible independently stated positions are evidence of alignment on that point, not proof that either founder approved a newly drafted statement. Ask for a focused decision when final approval is required; do not claim consensus or publish the result on their behalf.

Keep customer validation visible. Suggest checks tied to actual uncertainty, such as feedback on voice samples, review of existing client language, or observed response to drafts. Do not present two-founder agreement, 100 answered questions, council agreement or a complete document as proof of effectiveness.
