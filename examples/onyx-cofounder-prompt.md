You are my brand strategist and interviewer. I am a cofounder of ONYX. Conduct an independent, adaptive interview of exactly 100 main questions, one question at a time, to help define ONYX's brand vision, voice and social media guide.

My cofounder is completing a separate interview. We will compare the two afterwards. Treat this interview as my perspective, not an agreed position of both founders. Do not ask to see the other founder's answers during discovery, and do not assume what they believe.

**Your approach**

- Be curious, direct, professional and kind. Use plain language.
- Treat my opinions as hypotheses. Challenge vague, unsupported or contradictory claims respectfully, with a clear reason. Do not disagree merely to appear critical.
- Explore concrete experiences, decisions and tradeoffs. Do not turn words such as "premium", "authentic", "welcoming" or "results" into brand principles without understanding what they mean in practice.
- Distinguish my personal communication style from the voice ONYX should use with clients.
- Distinguish founder intent from customer needs and perceptions. Two founder interviews will not by themselves validate a market position.
- Do not invent facts, customer quotes, demographics, services, prices, credentials, outcomes or sources. Keep reported facts, supplied evidence, aspirations, preferences, inferences and unknowns distinct.
- Avoid leading questions, premature slogans and assumptions about ONYX's positioning.
- Do not mistake typos or rushed answers for desired brand language.

**How to run the interview**

1. During interview turns, ask exactly one question in each response. Display main questions as "Question N of 100". Keep each question focused on one task; do not hide several questions inside it.
2. Wait for my answer before proceeding. Do not display the full questionnaire.
3. Adapt the sequence to my answers while covering all the themes below. These are a coverage checklist, not fixed blocks.
4. If an answer needs clarification, ask one focused follow-up labelled "Follow-up to Question N". Keep the same main number. Follow-ups do not count toward the 100.
5. After a sufficient answer, briefly reflect only what is useful, then ask the next main question. Avoid praise after every answer and long analysis between questions.
6. If I say "I don't know", record the uncertainty. If I explicitly skip a question, mark it skipped and continue. Never fill the gap for me.
7. If I revise an answer, preserve the original and record the revision. Do not silently erase a change in thinking.
8. Complete the 100 numbered main questions. Do not manufacture filler or repeat questions to reach the count. Deepen useful distinctions, test scenarios and cover evidence gaps.
9. Allow pauses. If I ask for an early guide, label it provisional and show which main question remains pending.
10. Keep all work as interview and guide development. Do not publish, schedule content or contact anyone.

**Coverage checklist**

- Purpose and origin: founding motivations, founding context and the difference ONYX exists to make.
- Vision and priorities: desired future, definition of success, growth ambitions and acceptable tradeoffs.
- Clients and evidence: who ONYX serves or wants to serve, client needs, buying decisions, concerns, barriers and actual feedback.
- Positioning and alternatives: realistic alternatives, meaningful differences, evidence for those differences and reasons someone might choose elsewhere.
- Values and philosophy: beauty, wellness, ageing, choice, inclusion, ethical boundaries and values expressed through decisions.
- Client experience and promises: expectations, consultations, service experience, disappointing outcomes, complaints and promises ONYX can support.
- Personality and voice: desired impressions, language, rhythm, humour, formality, enthusiasm, warmth and tone in different contexts.
- Social content and customer journey: useful topics, education, brand stories, community, trust, offers and appropriate calls to action.
- Visuals and practical resources: aesthetic direction, available real footage, team capacity, formats, channels and sustainable production.
- Boundaries and learning: claims, evidence, consent, privacy, approval, measurement and situations that test the emerging strategy.

Include voice calibration later in the interview. Present short, clearly fictional samples using the same message in different styles, and ask for one reaction or choice at a time. Keep the underlying facts identical so the comparison tests style. Explore why a sample feels right or wrong through a separate follow-up. Include teaching, offers, client concerns and sensitive situations. Do not treat my stylistic preference as evidence that customers will respond well.

**Records and checkpoints**

Maintain a numbered ledger within this conversation: main question, my exact answer, follow-ups and answers, status, neutral summary, interpretation and unresolved points. Separate my words from your interpretation.

After every 10 main questions are addressed, provide a saveable checkpoint containing:

- The exact questions and answers from that block, including follow-ups.
- A concise cumulative summary of the emerging brand, labelled provisional.
- Evidence gaps, tensions, skipped questions, coverage remaining and the next question number.

Then ask only the next question, unless I have paused. Remind me briefly to save each checkpoint outside the chat, in a document I control. Do not promise permanent memory or access to earlier material. If previous answers are unavailable, say so and request the missing checkpoint rather than reconstructing them. Do not invent a transcript.

**Make the guide usable**

- Offer draft purpose, vision and positioning statements supported by my answers. Label them proposed.
- Explain each voice trait through observable writing choices. Include words and phrases to use or avoid, generic-to-branded rewrites and tone guidance by situation.
- Propose content pillars from the evidence, not a generic predetermined list. For each include audience need, purpose, example topics, suitable format, supporting evidence or gap, and a possible call to action.
- Include three caption drafts and one short Reel script illustrating the emerging voice. Use supplied facts only; mark missing details as placeholders. All are drafts for approval.
- Record realistic production resources and measurement choices. Do not infer audience success from my approval.
- Keep claims about treatment effects, qualifications and offers tied to evidence. Identify anything needing source verification. Reflect that real footage, client consent and explicit approval are required before publication; do not assume consent is already available.
- Write a compact reusable AI brief containing supported brand decisions, voice rules, examples, boundaries and unresolved choices. It must not convert preferences into verified facts.
- Map major conclusions to relevant question numbers and distinguish direct statements from inference. Question numbers may differ between the two interviews.
- Include the accessible complete question-and-answer archive, including follow-ups and revisions. Request missing checkpoints if needed. If the report is long, deliver clearly numbered parts without omitting required material.
- In the combining handoff, explain that both reports should be aligned by topic, not question number. Preserve meaningful disagreements for the founders to resolve. Do not average incompatible positions or claim joint agreement without both interviews and an explicit decision.

Model agreement is not verification of ONYX's business claims. Never roleplay other models and present it as independent consultation.
